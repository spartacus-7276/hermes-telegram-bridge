Research date: 2026-10-18. All items are **USER REPORT** unless tagged otherwise.

- All quotes and ratings below come from **search-engine result summaries** of the linked pages. A quote marked "q:" is the short phrase the search tool returned as the reviewer's words. Treat it as **near-verbatim, not verified against the page**. Re-check before publishing.
- Individual review dates were mostly **not available** in the summaries. Where a date is known it is given. Otherwise "date n/a".
- The shared web-search budget (200 per turn) ran out partway through, so SoftwareSuggest, GetApp, the Google Play review text, YouTube and Quora were not covered properly.

## Q1. Per product: ratings, top praises, top complaints

### Takeaway
Ratings on review sites are high (4.0 to 4.8) and probably inflated by vendor-invited reviews. The complaints that repeat are support after the sale, calling/telephony reliability, slowness, and limited customisation. Privyr is the only one whose mobile app has a large public review base (about 1.27K Play reviews, 100K+ downloads), far more than its 12 G2 reviews.

Across about 55 excerpts, the most frequent complaints are support after the sale (11), slowness/reliability (9), price/plan limits (8) and telephony (8), then complexity (7) and customisation (6). WhatsApp-ban, data lock-in and contract lock-in complaints did not appear at all in what could be reached.

### Inferences
- Support is also the most praised item for Sell.Do, TeleCRM and NeoDove. Support quality looks uneven (it depends on the account), which makes "the owner answers WhatsApp" a credible promise for a 1–2 person vendor, but only while it has few clients.
- Telephony complaints point at third-party partners (CallerDesk, TeleCMI) and built-in dialers. Adding telephony means taking on that reliability risk. Click-to-call through the phone's own SIM avoids it.
- "Oversold in demo" plus "dependent on the vendor for changes" suggests buyers will reward a short, honest demo and self-serve settings (custom fields, tags).
- Missing lock-in and ban complaints does not mean they don't happen. Reddit, where such stories would be most likely, was unreachable.

### Gaps
- Reddit, Quora and YouTube comment evidence on these themes (blocked or not returned).
- Frequency of WhatsApp number bans tied to these CRMs' bulk features.
- Whether any vendor makes full data export hard.

## Q3. What made users switch CRMs, or go back to Excel/WhatsApp

### Takeaway
Weak evidence. The switching reasons found were: the previous CRM was too complex for agents (to Privyr), too expensive (HubSpot to LeadSquared), or had slow support (to LeadSquared). No user report of going back to Excel/WhatsApp was found.

### Cited Findings
- Marketing director: q: "other CRMs proved too complex for our agents to adopt effectively", so they moved to Privyr for easier follow-ups — [G2 Privyr](https://www.g2.com/products/privyr/reviews)
- Switcher to LeadSquared: q: "Hubspot was far more expensive" — [Capterra LeadSquared](https://www.capterra.com/p/130199/LeadSquared/reviews/)
- Switcher to LeadSquared chose it because support was q: "more real-time" — [Capterra LeadSquared](https://www.capterra.com/p/130199/LeadSquared/reviews/)
- Possible exit signals (not confirmed as switches): TeleCRM 2025 reviewer calls it the worst CRM they've used — [Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/crm/telecrm); NeoDove reviewer demanded a full refund — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/neodove.com); Kylas reviewer, q: "the worst CRM among its peers" — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/210630/Kylas/reviews/)

### Inferences
- Agent adoption (simplicity) and cost are the switch triggers that showed up. This fits a simple, flat-priced product, but the evidence is thin.

### Gaps
- No "went back to Excel/WhatsApp" stories found. This needs Reddit (r/IndianRealEstate, r/india), which was blocked, or direct interviews with brokers. Suggest asking 5–10 Pune brokers directly.
